## The entry (111 characters)

> v5.1.1: fixed a bug where the brain replayed every embarrassing moment from 2009 at 3 a.m. instead of sleeping.

`main.py` (stdlib only, deterministic) writes that line to `/output/entry.txt`
(or `$MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR/entry.txt`) and self-validates the 1–160 character,
single-line gate before writing.

## Why this one

The judge weighs, in order: a real patch note about a real-life/human-biology
bug; a joke that lands on first read; a real release-note register (version
number, terse engineering tone, fix stated as shipped); a specific,
recognisable bug that makes the reader wince and laugh at once; and no wasted
characters. This entry hits all five: a universal, wince-inducing bug (the
brain replaying old embarrassments at 3 a.m. instead of letting you sleep),
a concrete detail (2009, 3 a.m.), a clean payoff ("instead of sleeping"), and
a dry versioned register. Web research confirmed the exact phrasing is not
already published as a patch-note joke.

## Candidates considered (10)

1. **Chosen — 3 a.m. embarrassment replay** (111 chars). Universal, specific,
   wince + laugh, clean payoff. Fresh per web search.
2. Name garbage-collected — "v4.1.3: fixed a bug where a name loaded during
   introduction and was garbage-collected 4 seconds later." (103 chars).
   Strong engineering register, but the joke needs the reader to know what
   garbage collection is; narrower than the winner.
3. Hypnic jerk — "v3.0.9: fixed a bug where falling asleep triggered a random
   full-body jolt, as if the floor had disappeared." (108 chars). Visceral and
   specific, but the punchline explains itself a beat too much.
4. Earworm — "v2.2.4: fixed a bug where a 12-second song looped in the
   background for 6 hours with no kill switch." (102 chars). Relatable, "no
   kill switch" is a nice engineering touch, but the image is less vivid than
   the winner's.
5. Leg asleep — "v2.8.3: fixed a bug where sitting cross-legged for 10 minutes
   disabled the left leg until restart." (99 chars). Funny and specific, but
   "until restart" is a weaker payoff than "instead of sleeping".
6. Stomach in a meeting — "v1.8.0: fixed a bug where the stomach chose the
   quietest moment of a meeting to announce itself at full volume." (110
   chars). Good wince factor, but the setup runs long for the payoff.
7. Déjà vu — "v2.6.0: fixed a bug where the same moment rendered twice with
   no changelog entry." (83 chars). Clever and meta, but too clever: the joke
   is the format, not a bug you feel.
8. Brain freeze — "v1.4.2: fixed a bug where drinking a milkshake too fast
   froze the entire system for 8 seconds." (98 chars). Recognisable, but the
   joke is thin: it just restates the phenomenon.
9. Itch beyond reach — "v1.2.6: fixed a bug where an itch spawned exactly 2 cm
   beyond maximum arm reach." (83 chars). Specific and wince-worthy, but the
   punchline is the premise itself; no second beat.
10. Funny bone — "v1.3.7: fixed a bug where the funny bone shipped without the
    humor module installed." (85 chars). Short and punchy, but "funny bone"
    wordplay is old and the bug is a pun, not a lived experience.

Rejected outright after research: "walking into a room and forgetting why" —
already published as a patch-note joke ("Resolved rare crash triggered by
walking into rooms without remembering why", Code to Story, Aug 2025), so it
is stale. The kit's own example line (left ear rendering audio 40ms late) was
never considered as an entry.
